Australian household energy

Know what's really running up your power bill.

Power Watch is a small demonstration site about appliance energy consumption in Australian homes. Electricity prices have climbed in recent years, and everyday appliances left running quietly in the background are often a bigger part of the bill than people expect. This page walks through the basics; the Televisions page lets you estimate the running cost of a TV for yourself.

Frequently asked questions

Click a question to reveal the answer.

Heating and cooling systems are usually the biggest contributor, followed by hot water and large appliances such as fridges and washing machines.

Most appliances list a wattage (W) or energy rating sticker. The higher the wattage, the more electricity the appliance draws while it's running.

Some devices continue to draw a small amount of standby power when left plugged in. Switching them off at the wall can lead to modest savings over a year.

A kWh is the appliance's power in watts, multiplied by the hours it runs, divided by 1000. Try it yourself on the Televisions page.
